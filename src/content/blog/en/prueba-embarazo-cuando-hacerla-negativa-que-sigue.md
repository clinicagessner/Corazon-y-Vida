Rocío is three days late. She bought two tests at the corner store, took one at night after getting home from work, and it came back negative. The next morning the second one said the same thing. Her period still hasn't come, her breasts feel tender, and she can't decide whether to trust the little box or her own body. We hear this question often, and there is nearly always an explanation. Here the medical team of Clínica Hispana Corazón y Vida walks you through when to take the test, why a negative result isn't always the final word, and which next steps make sense.

## What the test actually measures

Every pregnancy test looks for the same thing: the hormone hCG, which your body starts making once a pregnancy implants in the uterus. At first there is very little of it, and the amount climbs day by day. Almost everything else follows from that: if you test while the hormone is still low, the result may come back negative even though a pregnancy is there.

The two ways to test differ like this:

- **Urine:** both the drugstore kind and the one done at the clinic. It gives a yes or no, and you have the answer in a few minutes.
- **Blood (quantitative hCG):** measures how much hormone is present. It picks up a pregnancy a few days before a urine test can, and two samples can be compared to see whether the number is rising as expected.

## When to test so the result is useful

The clinic's [pregnancy test](/services/prueba-embarazo) page puts it simply: a urine test detects pregnancy from the first day of a missed period, and before that it can give a false negative. This table helps you pick the timing and the type of test:

| Your situation | Test that usually helps most | Practical tip |
|---|---|---|
| Your period isn't late yet | Blood, if you need to know early | A urine test can easily read negative even with a pregnancy |
| First day late or later | Urine | Best with first morning urine, which is more concentrated |
| Irregular cycles, unsure when it's due | Blood, or a repeat urine test | Counting "days late" is hard when the date shifts every month |
| Very faint line or unclear result | Blood | It gives a number and doesn't depend on reading a line |

The blood test needs no fasting and can be drawn at any time of day.

## Why it can be negative when your period doesn't come

A negative test with a late period has two possible explanations: either the test missed a pregnancy that is really there (a false negative), or there truly is no pregnancy and something else is delaying your cycle. Let's start with the first.

**Common reasons for a false negative:**

1. **You tested too early.** This is the top cause. If you ovulated later than usual that month, the pregnancy is younger than you think and the hormone isn't high enough to show up yet.
2. **Diluted urine.** Testing at night, after drinking lots of water or soda, lowers the concentration of the hormone in the sample.
3. **Reading at the wrong time.** Checking the window before the time printed in the instructions can hide a line that was about to appear. Reading it long afterward can show marks that don't count.
4. **An expired or poorly stored test.** A box that spent months in a hot car or a humid bathroom may not work properly.
5. **Miscounting how late you are.** If your cycles run five or six weeks, "a week late" may really be the day your period was barely due.

If you suspect a false negative, the sensible move is to repeat the urine test in a few days with first morning urine, or to get the blood test, which settles the question with a number.

## If the negative is real: other reasons for a late period

When testing confirms there is no pregnancy and your period still doesn't arrive, your body is usually signaling something else. The most common causes are:

- **Heavy stress or a change in routine:** a move, night shifts, travel or a loss in the family can shift ovulation.
- **Weight changes:** losing or gaining several pounds quickly, or exercising much more than usual.
- **Breastfeeding:** while nursing, it's normal for periods to take a while to return or to come irregularly.
- **Birth control:** some methods, like the injection, can space periods far apart or stop them for a while; after stopping, the cycle also takes time to settle.
- **Thyroid and prolactin:** when the [thyroid](/services/tiroides) gland runs too fast or too slow, or prolactin is high, cycles get disrupted.
- **Approaching menopause:** after 40, longer or skipped cycles are common.

At the clinic, if the test is negative and your period doesn't come, the next step is looking for the cause of the delay and, when needed, ordering thyroid or prolactin tests. If late periods keep happening month after month, or come with pain, bleeding at odd times or unusual discharge, a [gynecology](/services/ginecologia) visit includes a pelvic exam and, when the uterus and ovaries need a look, an ultrasound. If any result comes back abnormal, a specialized evaluation is a good idea.

## Negative and relieved: a good time to talk about birth control

For many women a late period is a wake-up call: they didn't want a pregnancy right now and realize their method isn't working for them, or that they aren't using one at all. That scare is a good moment to choose one calmly.

The clinic's [birth control](/services/anticonceptivos) page explains that the pill or the three-month injection can be started at the clinic, and that for the implant or an intrauterine device we guide you on where to have it placed. Before starting, if there is any doubt, a pregnancy test is done to make sure the method begins at the right time. If your chosen method needs a prescription, it is sent electronically to the pharmacy you choose, whichever one is on your way home or to work.

## Signs that mean going straight to the ER

With a positive test, or if you might be pregnant even though a test was negative, don't wait or book a visit if you have:

- Severe pain on one side of your abdomen or pelvis, especially if it keeps getting worse.
- Vaginal bleeding, particularly if it is heavy or comes with pain.
- Strong lightheadedness, pale and clammy skin, or passing out.
- Pain at the tip of your shoulder along with belly pain.

These symptoms can point to a pregnancy growing outside the uterus. Call 911 or go to an emergency room right away.
