A man in his mid-forties comes home from work with a dull ache "down there," between the testicles and the anus. The next day it burns when he pees, the stream comes out halfway, and by night his whole body aches. Most men blame a strain, a hernia or "something with the kidneys," and wait to see if it goes away. Quite often the source is a gland almost nobody talks about until it hurts: the prostate. When it becomes inflamed, the problem is called prostatitis, and it is worth understanding its different forms, because they are not all treated the same way and not all of them can wait.

## A small gland in an awkward spot

The prostate is roughly the size of a walnut and wraps around the urethra just below the bladder. So when it swells, it squeezes the channel urine flows through, and because of where it sits, the pain is felt in the perineum (the area between the scrotum and the anus), the lower back, the groin or the tip of the penis. Unlike benign prostate enlargement, which usually shows up after fifty, prostatitis can affect young and middle-aged men; according to the National Institutes of Health (NIH), it is one of the most frequent reasons for urinary visits in men under fifty.

## Four kinds of prostatitis, four different responses

| Type | How it shows up | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Acute bacterial | Starts suddenly: fever, chills, intense burning, pelvic pain and trouble urinating | Prompt care; if you cannot urinate or the fever is high, the ER |
| Chronic bacterial | Urine infections that come back again and again, with milder discomfort in between | Study the urine with a culture to identify the bacteria |
| Chronic pelvic pain | Pain or pressure in the pelvis for months, with no bacteria in the tests | Symptom and habit management; antibiotics alone do not solve it |
| Inflammatory without symptoms | Found by chance on a lab test | Interpret the result calmly before worrying |

The chronic pelvic pain form is actually the most common one, and the most frustrating, because the man feels that "the tests come back fine" while the ache is still there.

## Prostatitis, bladder infection or an enlarged prostate?

All three share burning and frequent trips to the bathroom, so they are easy to mix up. A few clues help you get your bearings, though they do not replace an exam:

- **Bladder infection:** burning and urgency, sometimes cloudy or foul-smelling urine; in men it is less common than in women, and when it appears, the prostate deserves a look.
- **Acute bacterial prostatitis:** the same, plus fever, shaking, pain behind the scrotum and a feeling that the bladder never fully empties.
- **Benign prostate enlargement:** a weak stream, dribbling at the end and getting up several times a night, settling in gradually, without fever or sharp pain.

If you also notice blood in your urine or semen, mention it at the visit even if it happened only once.

## Treatment: why leftover pills do not do the job

When bacteria are involved, prostatitis is treated with an antibiotic chosen by the medical team based on your symptoms and, if a culture was sent, adjusted to the result. The prostate is a tissue that not every medication reaches well, so taking what was left over from another infection, or what a buddy recommended, may ease things for a few days and leave the bacteria hiding, ready to come back. Take it completely and exactly as explained, even if you feel better after a few days.

Alongside treatment, simple measures are usually recommended: drink water spread through the day, do not hold it when you need to go, take warm sitz baths, and cut back for a few days on alcohol, coffee and very spicy food, which irritate the bladder.

## When not to wait for an office visit

Some situations call for 911 or going straight to the emergency room, not the clinic:

- For hours you have felt your bladder full and cannot get the urine out, or only drops come.
- The thermometer shows a high fever, you are shaking with chills, and it also burns or hurts to urinate.
- You feel confused, very weak or have a racing heart along with those symptoms.

An acute prostate infection can reach the bloodstream, and trapped urine needs to be relieved right away. In those cases, do not drive yourself if you feel unwell.

## When the pain lasts for months

Chronic pelvic pain syndrome shows up in men who spend long hours sitting (drivers, equipment operators, desk jobs), during stressful stretches, or after an infection that has already cleared. It is not cancer and, according to the NIH, there is no evidence that prostatitis raises the risk of prostate cancer. What it does do is affect sleep, mood and life as a couple.

What usually helps is a combination, not a single pill:

- Get up and walk every hour if you work behind the wheel or at a desk, and use a cushion with a hollow center.
- Local heat or warm baths when the ache flares.
- Figure out what makes it worse: for some it is spicy food, for others coffee, beer or riding a bike.
- Look at stress and rest, because tension in the pelvic floor muscles keeps the pain going.
- Keep your glucose in check if you have diabetes, since high sugar favors repeat infections; we follow that in [chronic condition management](/services/condiciones-cronicas).
